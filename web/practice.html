          Document architecture
          Five families, distinct formats
          
          
          Transactional: agreements, wills, trusts and deeds require party capacity, operative clauses, consideration or property, execution and registration checks.
          
          
          Litigation: the four workflows above have separate criminal, police and civil procedural structures. Petitions, plaints, appeals, revisions, witness/evidence applications and settlements need their own schemas.
          
          
          Legislative: Bills and ordinances need competence, enabling basis, clause structure, commencement, repeal and delegated-power review.
          
          
          Policy: schemes, guidelines and government notices need authority, eligibility, implementation, grievance and impact checks; a statutory notification may belong with legislative drafting.
          
          
          Research: the working legal opinion above separates its question, source facts, assumptions, dated primary authorities, contrary law, analysis and bounded conclusion.
          
          
          A memorandum of understanding is normally transactional. An instrument’s legal effect and forum determine its schema, not its title alone. Additional instruments are mapped in the design report and require their own validated workflows.
